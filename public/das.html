Debris Assessment Software (DAS) analysis

NASA's Debris Assessment Software (DAS 3.2.7) checks a mission against NASA-STD-8719.14, and its results go into the Orbital Debris Assessment Report that accompanies the FCC filing. This page gives our pre-DAS analysis, calibrated against real DAS runs for PROVES 1U satellites, and includes a ready-made DAS project for this spacecraft.

Key finding. A bare 1U at the Transporter-class altitude of 525 km takes about 7 years to reenter. That meets NASA's 25-year requirement but not the FCC 5-year rule (FCC 22-74, applying to satellites launched after 29 Sep 2024). Options:

  • A drop-off at or below about 475 km, for example from the rideshare's orbital transfer vehicle.
  • A small deployable drag sail. Doubling the area roughly halves the lifetime.
  • An FCC waiver argument.

This drives a launch-provider requirement and should be resolved before the ODAR is filed.

Model and calibration against DAS

The model integrates circular-orbit decay, da/dt = −√(μa)·ρ·CDA/m, with CD = 2.2 (as in DAS) and a piecewise-exponential atmosphere blended between solar minimum and maximum on an 11-year cycle (Cycle 25 maximum about 2025.0). A single density scale factor (0.439) was fitted so the model reproduces DAS 3.x lifetimes for the PROVES 1U satellites in The-PROVES-Project-DAS:

The calibration points are all near 417 km and near solar maximum, so values at 500–600 km through the coming solar minimum are extrapolations. The DAS run itself is the authoritative result.
