Power budget

The beacon can run continuously; the transponder needs scheduling

The spacecraft is a tumbling 1U with body-mounted silicon cells on five faces, so energy is the limiting resource. The 48 Wh battery is large compared with any single eclipse. What limits operations is the orbit-average energy balance.

1. Solar generation

Each face carries 6 cells at about 0.20 W (AM1.5), giving 1.64 W per face at AM0. The cells are on five faces (±X, ±Y, −Z); +Z is the antenna cap. For a randomly tumbling cube each face averages |cos θ| = ¼, so the mean sunlit input is 1.25 face-equivalents = 2.05 W before losses.

3. How much transponder time?

Result. At worst case the transponder can run about 69 minutes per day. That's enough for roughly 7 ten-minute passes over high-demand regions. Commanded or battery-gated scheduling is required. Ways to increase transponder time:

  • Triple-junction cells (about 29 % vs about 22 % for Si) would add about 30 %.
  • Higher-β orbits (dawn-dusk SSO) give up to 26 % duty.
  • Cells on the +Z cap.
  • Reducing LTM output power at low uplink load.

Assumptions to verify

  • The cell Pmp of the KXOB101K08TF (datasheet) and a measured face I-V curve under AM0-equivalent light.
  • The LT3652 operating point with a 2S face (see open items).
  • LTM current in each mode, measured on the FlatSat through the FC's TLM3 (LTM_VSYS) channel and INA219.
  • The orbit and LTAN from the Transporter-22 manifest, which set the β-angle history.
