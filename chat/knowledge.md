## Flying the AMSAT Linear Transponder Module on a PROVES Kit 1U
DPI LTM CubeSat puts AMSAT's flight-proven V/u Linear Transponder Module (LTM-1) on an open-source
PROVES Kit 1U bus, with a modified PROVES V5d flight controller that drives the LTM over CAN. When it's in orbit, amateur radio
operators around the world will be able to make SSB and CW contacts through a 30 kHz passband. The 1200 bps BPSK
telemetry beacon will be received by the global FoxTelem ground network.

## Mission objectives
Demonstrate the AMSAT LTM on an open-source university bus. The LTM's command, telemetry and transponder functions run on a PROVES Kit spacecraft instead of an AMSAT Fox bus.
Provide a public V/u linear transponder (SSB/CW, 30 kHz passband) for the worldwide amateur-satellite community.
Return health and science telemetry on a 1200 bps BPSK beacon decoded by FoxTelem (https://www.amsat.org/foxtelem-software-for-windows-mac-linux/), including host temperatures and bus data that the flight controller sends to the LTM over CAN.
Validate the FC V5d LTM interface board (CAN, switched 5 V LTM supply, umbilical and telemetry lines) as a reusable path for other PROVES users who want to fly the LTM.

Spacecraft
## A PROVES Kit 1U bus with an AMSAT LTM payload
The spacecraft is built almost entirely from open-source PROVES Kit hardware. The one custom board is a
PROVES V5d flight controller with its on-board radios removed and replaced by an interface to the LTM.
## Subsystems
## Payload: AMSAT LTM
A V/u linear transponder (SSB/CW, 30 kHz passband) with a 1200 bps BPSK telemetry beacon and a command receiver.
It is a three-board PC-104 stack (transponder at the bottom, the ICR command receiver in the middle, and the LTI host interface on top), 92 × 90 × 33 mm, 130 g, running on 5–7 V.
It has flight heritage on HuskySat-1, Fox-1E and MESAT1.
amsat.org → LTM (https://www.amsat.org/amsat-linear-transponder-module/)
## Flight controller: FC V5d LTM
RP2350 running CircuitPython, with an LSM6DSO IMU, LIS2MDL magnetometer, RTC and SD NAND. The RFM98/E28 radios are replaced by an
MCP25625 CAN controller (1 Mbit/s) and a switched 5 V LTM supply that stays off at boot and is enabled after
antenna deployment. The board also carries umbilical sense, console and telemetry lines and a host temperature sensor.
FC_V5d_LTM_mods_20261010 (https://github.com/dpiresearch/claude_flight_board/tree/main/hardware/FC_V5d_LTM_mods_20261010) · firmware / ltm1 driver (https://github.com/dpiresearch/claude_flight_board/tree/main/firmware)
## EPS
The EPS sits on the flight controller: an LT3652 2S solar charger (8.38 V float, 1 A), a TPS54226 3.3 V rail, the TPS54225 5 V
LTM rail, INA219 battery and solar monitors, per-face load switches, and TPS4H160 burn-wire and heater drivers.
## Battery pack v2
Four 18650 Li-ion cells in 2S2P (NCR18650B class, about 48 Wh at 7.2 V), with an R5460 2-cell protector,
back-to-back FETs and four TMP112 temperature sensors.
battery_pack_v2 (https://github.com/proveskit/battery_board/tree/main/battery_pack_v2)
## Solar: XY Faces V2a
Six KXOB101K08TF mono-Si cells per face in 2s3p, with Schottky-isolated strings. Each panel also carries a
MCP9808 temperature sensor, a VEML7700 light sensor and a DRV2605L-driven PCB magnetorquer coil.
About 1.6 W per face at normal incidence (AM0, BOL).
XY_Faces_V2a (https://github.com/proveskit/solar_boards/tree/main/XY_Faces_V2a)
## Antenna top cap v2c
A +Z endcap with burn-wire-released tape-measure elements, a CSD17576 burn FET, a balun, an MMCX feed and
sun/temperature sensors.
antenna_top_cap_v2c (https://github.com/proveskit/antenna-board/tree/main/antenna_top_cap_v2c)
## Structure
The PROVES 1U frame is made of 1.6 mm 5052 aluminium sheet with PA-12 feet, M2.5 self-clinching fasteners and normally-closed
deployment switches. It has been vibration-tested to 20 g RMS and thermally cycled.
PROVES documentation (https://github.com/proveskit/Documentation)
## ADCS
Passive and magnetic. B-dot detumble uses the face PCB coils and the torque rods/air coil from the PROVES build. No pointing is
required: the transponder and beacon work while the spacecraft tumbles, at the cost of the antenna-null allowance in the link budget.

## Open design items
1. V-band (2 m) receive antenna. The antenna top cap's tape elements are about 0.18 m long, which is a 70 cm quarter-wave.
The LTM's uplink is in the 144 MHz band and needs its own element (about 0.5 m quarter-wave) or a diplexed dual-band arrangement.
The link budget assumes a dedicated V-band monopole.
2. Charger operating point. The LT3652 input-regulation threshold (about 8.44 V) and startup headroom need to be checked
on the bench against the 2S panel's Vmp and Voc at hot and cold temperatures. The power budget carries a 10 % operating-point loss for this.
3. FC V5d LTM layout. Parts are placed but not routed (87 ratsnest connections). The J30–LTM harness has not been designed yet.
4. LTM interface details. CAN termination, the host temperature-sensor address and health-message encoding all need to be confirmed with AMSAT.
The interface follows AMSAT's partner documentation, which is not reproduced on this site.
5. Part-number reconciliation. The solar cell part (KXOB101K08TF vs SM101K07TF) and the battery protector variant differ between
README, schematic and footprint in the source repositories.

## How the LTM-1 sits in the bus
The stack, bottom to top: 18650 battery pack (lowest, to keep the centre of mass low), then the FC V5d LTM flight controller, then the
AMSAT LTM-1. The LTM's three boards stack through a PC-104 connector: transponder at the bottom, then the ICR command receiver,
then the LTI host interface on top. That puts the RF board's coax leads close to the +Z antenna cap.
Interfaces: the J30 harness (grey) carries the switched 5 V LTM_VSYS supply, CAN at 1 Mbit/s, I2C, umbilical sense and telemetry lines between the FC and the LTI.
The LTI's USB Mini-B umbilical faces a side panel for ground access.
Antennas: the PROVES top cap's 2 × 178 mm tape elements form the 70 cm (432 MHz) downlink dipole. The orange 2 m element is a
proposed addition for the 144 MHz uplink, an open design item (see Spacecraft → open items).
Fidelity: board outlines, the stack order and the envelope follow AMSAT's public LTM description and the PROVES CAD dimensions.
Component placement on each board is illustrative, not the flight layout.

## Mission phases
0 · Integration & test Pre-launch
FlatSat with the LTM on the bench, powered from the USB umbilical (which forces LTM_VSYS on). AMSAT's LTM acceptance test procedure is run, followed by flight-software qualification of the ltm1 CAN driver and environmental tests (random vibration, thermal vacuum and thermal cycling). The spacecraft is delivered with its RBF pin and with the deployment switches holding it powered off.
1 · Launch L-0
Integrated in a 1U slot of a rideshare dispenser on SpaceX Transporter-22. Deployment switches and inhibits keep the battery disconnected, so there is no RF and no power during ascent.
2 · Deployment & quiet period T+0 → T+30 min
The deployment switches close and the RP2350 boots. It logs the boot count, starts the RTC and keeps the LTM rail OFF (R125 pull-down). No deployables fire and no RF is transmitted for 30 minutes. Magnetic B-dot detumble starts using the face coils.
3 · Antenna deployment T+30 min
The burn wire is fired through the TPS4H160 DEPLOY channel (about 1–2 A for a few seconds). The deployment flag is set in non-volatile memory. If the first burn fails, the software retries on later orbits with a sunlit-battery check.
4 · LTM power-on & first beacon T+45 min
Flight software enables LTM_VSYS through MCP23017 GPB6, which is allowed only once antennas are deployed. The LTM boots in safe mode and starts its 1200 bps BPSK telemetry beacon. The FC begins sending health data (temperatures, UTC) over CAN.
5 · Commissioning Week 1 – 4
The AMSAT command station checks LTM and bus health. FoxTelem stations worldwide upload frames to the AMSAT telemetry server. Team members verify the power and thermal trends against this site's budgets, then run short scheduled transponder tests.
6 · Nominal operations Month 1 → EOL
The beacon runs continuously. The transponder is power-limited to about 5–8 % duty (about 69 min/day at β = 0°). It is scheduled over high-demand regions (North America, Europe, Japan) and weekend events, with a battery-gated fallback (ON above 80 % state of charge, OFF below 70 %).
7 · Safe mode Any time
Triggered by low state of charge, a watchdog reset, an over- or under-temperature condition or a ground command. The LTM drops to safe mode (40 mA), the FC slows its clock, and the battery heater is thermostatic. Recovery is by ground command once the battery has charged.
8 · End of mission & disposal ~7 yr at 525 km
On ground command the LTM transmitter is permanently disabled (FCC requires the ability to cease emissions). The spacecraft decays passively, and DAS shows every component demises on reentry.

## Ground segment
## AMSAT command station(s)
High-gain stations uplink commands to the LTM command receiver (20 kHz FSK). This link has
more than 30 dB margin at 10° elevation.
## FoxTelem network
Volunteer stations decode the 1200 bps BPSK beacon with FoxTelem (https://www.amsat.org/foxtelem-software-for-windows-mac-linux/) and forward
frames to AMSAT's server, so coverage is global rather than limited to a single team ground station.
## Amateur users
Users work SSB and CW through the 30 kHz V/u passband. Uplink Doppler is ±3.4 kHz and downlink
±10.2 kHz, so full-duplex Doppler tracking (for example with SatPC32 or Gpredict) is recommended.
## Mission team
Reviews telemetry trends, plans transponder schedules and coordinates with AMSAT operations.

Each face carries 6 cells at about 0.20 W (AM1.5), giving

## Assumptions
Spacecraft RF (AMSAT LTM (https://www.amsat.org/amsat-linear-transponder-module/)): 100 mW telemetry only, 450 mW telemetry plus transponder. The transponder's
350 mW is shared among 5 simultaneous SSB users.
Spacecraft antennas: −3 dBi effective for a tumbling monopole or dipole (an allowance for pattern nulls) and 1 dB line loss.
The 2 m element is TBD (see open items).
Spacecraft receive noise: 700 K for an Earth-facing VHF antenna (about 290 K Earth plus man-made noise) and the LNA.
Propagation: 3 dB polarization loss (linear spacecraft vs circular ground, or Faraday fading) and 0.5 dB atmospheric and ionospheric loss.
Required SNR: 10 dB in 2.4 kHz for SSB, 6 dB in 500 Hz for CW. BPSK needs Eb/N0 of 9.6 dB (uncoded 10⁻⁵) plus 1.5 dB implementation loss.
This is conservative; FoxTelem's FEC improves on it.
Ground gains are typical amateur values (handheld 2 m / 70 cm Yagi, crossed Yagis with LNA) and have not been checked against vendor data.

## Debris Assessment Software (DAS) analysis
NASA's Debris Assessment Software (https://orbitaldebris.jsc.nasa.gov/mitigation/debris-assessment-software.html) (DAS 3.2.7) checks a mission against NASA-STD-8719.14, and its results go into the
Orbital Debris Assessment Report that accompanies the FCC filing. This page gives our pre-DAS analysis, calibrated against real
DAS runs for PROVES 1U satellites, and includes a ready-made DAS project for this spacecraft.

Key finding. A bare 1U at the Transporter-class altitude of 525 km takes about 7 years to reenter. That meets NASA's 25-year
requirement but not the FCC 5-year rule (FCC 22-74, applying to satellites launched after 29 Sep 2024). Options:
A drop-off at or below about 475 km, for example from the rideshare's orbital transfer vehicle.
A small deployable drag sail. Doubling the area roughly halves the lifetime.
An FCC waiver argument.
This drives a launch-provider requirement and should be resolved before the ODAR is filed.

## Model and calibration against DAS
The model integrates circular-orbit decay, da/dt = −√(μa)·ρ·CDA/m, with CD = 2.2 (as in DAS) and a piecewise-exponential
atmosphere blended between solar minimum and maximum on an 11-year cycle (Cycle 25 maximum about 2025.0). A single density scale factor
(0.439) was fitted so the model reproduces DAS 3.x lifetimes for the PROVES 1U satellites in
The-PROVES-Project-DAS (https://github.com/proveskit/The-PROVES-Project-DAS):
DAS reference case (413 × 422 km, 51.6°) | DAS (yr) | Model (yr) | Error |

The calibration points are all near 417 km and near solar maximum, so values at 500–600 km through the coming solar minimum are
extrapolations. The DAS run itself is the authoritative result.
